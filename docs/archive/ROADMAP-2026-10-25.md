## 1. How to use this file

### The rules

1. **Exactly one milestone is active.** Work top to bottom inside it. Don't
   start the next milestone until the current one's **Done when** is true.
2. **New idea mid-task?** One dated line in the **Parking lot**, then back to
   work. Don't open a branch for it, don't research it, don't prototype it.
3. **Every task has a size.**
   - **S** = under half a day
   - **M** = 1–2 days
   - **L** = 3–5 days
   - Anything bigger than L gets split before it starts.
4. **Every task has an ID** (`M1.5`) so commits, Claude Code prompts and
   `annoyances.md` lines can point at it.
5. **This file is a plan, not a spec.** It says *what* and *why* and *how
   you'll know it's done*. It does not freeze implementation details. If a
   task needs more than a short paragraph of explanation, it isn't understood
   yet: do a spike first, write down what you learned, then size it.
6. **Cut from the bottom.** On a cut-check date, cut the lowest unfinished
   items in the active milestone. Items marked **never cut** stay.
7. **Review this file only between milestones.** Mid-milestone, you only tick
   boxes and add Parking lot lines.

### Status markers

- `[ ]` not started
- `[~]` in progress (only one at a time)
- `[x]` done (all acceptance checks pass, `just ci` green, pushed)
- `[-]` cut (leave the line, add the date and why)

### Tags used in the feature catalogue

- **v0.1** — in the 8 November preview
- **v1.0** — in the 13 December public launch
- **Next** — first things after v1.0, in order
- **Later** — worth doing, not scheduled
- **Never** — decided against, with a reason (see §28)

## 2. Who Hogwash is for

Being specific about who it's for is what stops the app from becoming
everything. When a feature idea comes up, ask: *which of these people needs
it, today?*

### Primary: the self-taught adult with a digital piano (you)

- Owns a MIDI-capable digital piano (Roland FP-10 class), plays at home.
- Can sort of read notation (landmark notes) and wants to get properly fluent.
- Learns specific pieces they care about, not graded exercises.
- Practises 15–45 minutes, irregularly, usually in the evening.
- Frustrations: doesn't know *what* to practise; plays the whole piece
  start-to-finish every time; the hard bars stay hard; apps either teach
  key-pressing (Synthesia) or put everything behind a paywall.
- Uses Linux or is comfortable installing software.

### Secondary: the returning player

- Had lessons as a kid, stopped, came back as an adult.
- Reads reasonably well; rusty.
- Wants a structured way to bring old pieces back and find weak spots.
- Values a calm, serious tool over a game.

### Tertiary: the student between lessons

- Has a teacher once a week, practises alone in between.
- Benefits from "here are the 3 bars you struggled with this week," which
  they can show their teacher.
- Not a teacher-facing product in v1 (no assignments, no accounts).

### Not for (and that's fine)

- Complete beginners who can't find middle C: they need lessons and
  flashcards (MidiSlayer, Simply Piano, books).
- People who want falling notes: Synthesia and Neothesia exist.
- People without a MIDI keyboard: the computer-keyboard fallback is for
  trying the app, not for practising.
- Non-piano instruments.

## 3. Product rules (and why each exists)

These are settled. Reopen one only with new evidence, and log it in §32.

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **Two screens: Library and Practice.** Settings, history and help are panels, not tabs. | MidiSlayer has eight top-level tabs and its identity got lost in them. A practice tool should open straight into practice. |
| **Pieces, not drills.** No flashcards, interval trainers, theory lessons, exams or games. | That's MidiSlayer's lane; they're far ahead and charge for it. Depth in one lane beats breadth in five. |
| **No gamification.** No XP, ranks, levels, streaks, points or badges. | It optimises for opening the app, not for getting better. Motivation should come from watching a bar improve. |
| **Factual feedback.** Location + measurement, never praise or judgment. | "Bar 9: stalled 4 s, 2 wrong notes" is useful. "Great job!" is noise, and "You struggled" stings. |
| **The cursor waits.** Wait mode is the core experience. | It's what makes reading practice possible at your own speed. Tempo comes later, as a graduation step. |
| **Local-first.** No accounts, no network, no telemetry. | Your practice history is personal. It also means nothing to host, nothing to break. |
| **Everything is free.** No locked features, ever. | MidiSlayer charges $50 to play a public-domain Bach prelude. Being the free version is the point. |
| **Users bring their own music.** Hogwash bundles only lawfully redistributable scores. | Shipping copyrighted arrangements gets a repo taken down. |
| **Integration over invention.** Verovio engraves, midir talks MIDI, MusicXML is the source of truth. | Every hour spent rebuilding a solved problem is an hour not spent on weak bars. |
| **Deterministic analysis.** Same performance + same score = byte-identical results. | It's what makes the engine testable without a piano, and it's already enforced in CI. Don't lose it. |

## 4. The landscape: competitors and what we take from each

### MidiSlayer (closest competitor) — studied hands-on, 6 Oct

**What it is:** a desktop sight-reading trainer by Monoglyph Ltd (UK).
$50 one-time, proprietary. Linux AppImage/.deb/.rpm, Windows, macOS.
69 public-domain pieces (4 playable free), MusicXML import, nine drill modes,
seven reading lessons, a daily game, ranks/XP/streaks, analytics.

**Its centre is drills, not pieces.** The home screen's main button is
"Begin: Note Flashcards." Practising a piece is one tab of eight. Its
analytics (note weakness map, slowest notes, exam score, first-try accuracy)
are all about reading single notes, not about bars of a piece.

**Adopt (take the idea, build it our way):**
- One-row practice toolbar: title + position counter ("1 / 561"), hands
  selector, Wait/Tempo toggle, tempo stepper, Listen, Loop, zoom, library.
- Current position highlighted as a soft vertical band through both staves.
- Library cards styled like sheet-music covers: cream paper, serif italic
  title, composer in small caps, a one-line musical hook
  ("G major · 3/4 · two hands").
- Pieces grouped by level ("Beginner — first tunes", "Intermediate — two
  hands, Grades 1–4").
- Device indicator always visible, top right: green dot + device name.
- Computer-keyboard fallback (A–K plays C4–C5) with an on-screen keyboard.
- Keyboard range: "From A0 to C8 · 88 notes", with a Detect button.
- Empty states that tell you what to do next ("Complete a session to see
  your weak spots").
- Onboarding strip when no device: 1 Connect your keyboard → 2 Select it →
  3 You're ready.
- Engraving with fingering numbers, tempo marking, expression text visible.

**Adapt (good idea, wrong framing for us):**
- *Consistency calendar* → a factual practice log per piece (dates and
  minutes), without streak counts. See decision D6.
- *Focus note / weak spots* → focus **bar**, computed from real pieces.
- *Graded library* → grade labels as plain description, not a progression
  system you "unlock."
- *Tip of the day* → contextual one-line hints in empty states only.

**Avoid (what we saw go wrong):**
- Eight tabs; Progress and Analytics overlapping.
- "PRO" badges on 8 of 9 cards: the free tier feels like a shop window.
- A raw `UnhandledPromise: Failed to start the audio device` error toast on
  every screen. Errors must never leak internals.
- Built-in software synth that fails on Linux (PipeWire/ALSA): we send MIDI
  out to the user's piano instead.
- Ranks ("Novice"), points per minute, XP levels, practice streaks.

### Other tools

| Tool | What it is | Take | Leave |
|---|---|---|---|
| **midipiano.app** | Free browser app; marks notes on the score as you play; loop, slow tempo, hands separately, wait mode; MusicXML/MXL/MEI import | Proof that free + sheet music + wait mode is wanted | Browser-only, no history across sessions |
| **Synthesia** | Paid, falling notes + sheet view from MIDI; last release Dec 2022 | Loop and hands-separate conventions users already know | Falling notes as the primary view |
| **Neothesia** | Open-source Rust falling-notes visualiser | Nothing directly; clean-room anyway (GPL) | Its scoring code that nobody reads; per-frame MIDI polling |
| **Soundslice** | Paid web player + OMR scanner + editor | Proof that PDF scanning is a hard, funded, multi-year problem | Trying to compete on scanning |
| **playsheet** | Free web; sheet + MIDI; rhythm-game scoring | Session review idea | Proprietary service, data not local, game scoring |
| **AnyScore** | iPad/iPhone; wait-for-me; weakest measures across pieces | **Validation of the weak-bars idea** | Apple-only, closed |
| **MuseScore Studio** | Free notation editor; MIDI import; MusicXML export | Where users get MusicXML; its MIDI importer via webmscore (post-v1) | — |

### Lessons from projects that walked this road

- **MuseScore** (Werner Schweer, 2002): built for himself, nearly lost
  interest after v1 worked, paused, came back, and outlasted Finale.
  Its co-founder's lesson: they *couldn't win on price*, only on product
  quality. **For us: "free" gets people to try it; "it actually tells me
  which bar to practise" is what keeps them.**
- **Lichess** (Thibault Duplessis, 2010): a throwaway project kept alive by
  one stranger who used it and complained. **For us: motivation comes from
  the first real user, not from more planning.** Get one other pianist using
  Hogwash as early as M4.

## 6. Technical facts that shape the work

These come straight from the code and decide how big several tasks are.

- **`ObservedPerformance` stores only pitches**, in order
  (`crates/hogwash-capture/src/observed.rs`). No time, no velocity. Both
  MIDI callbacks ignore midir's timestamp (`hogwash-midi/src/keyboard.rs`,
  `_timestamp`). → M1.1 changes the core data type; records format changes
  with it.
- **The `Cursor` can only start at the beginning** (`Cursor::at_the_beginning`).
  There is no seek. → Start-from-bar and looping need a new constructor or
  method that places the cursor at the first position of a given bar,
  respecting repeats (a bar can appear twice in played order).
- **Analysis has no per-bar rollup.** `finding.rs` says so explicitly: results
  carry a location, but aggregation per measure was deferred. → M2.1 adds it.
- **`AttemptRecord` is `{ played, reached }`.** Per-bar stats can be
  *recomputed* from (score, played events) at any time because analysis is
  pure. → Decide in M2.1 whether to store stats or recompute (recommendation:
  recompute, cache in memory; store only raw events + timestamps).
- **`SessionRecord.started` is a caller-supplied string**, never read from a
  clock inside the domain. Keep that discipline for timestamps: the MIDI
  adapter supplies time as data.
- **Feedback lines are built per finding** (`hogwash-feedback`), which is why
  an early stop produces hundreds of lines. → M1.4 groups them.
- **Marks are addressed by measure + position + page** (`Mark` in
  `bridge/types.ts`). The heatmap can tint per measure using the same SVG
  geometry the cursor already uses.
- **Verovio is a runtime `.so` from a sibling checkout.** The release build
  must bundle it (LGPL, separate library). This is the single biggest
  release risk.
- **Engine crates must stay pure** (`scripts/check-arch.sh`). New logic for
  looping, hands and per-bar stats goes in `hogwash-practice` and
  `hogwash-analysis`, never in the Tauri layer.

## 10. M1 — The practice loop actually loops

**Goal:** practising a piece feels like practising, not like taking a test.
**Time:** ~1.5 weeks.
**Done when:** you can open the Minuet, jump to bar 9, loop bars 9–12 with
your right hand only, see "3 clean in a row," and go back to the library —
touching nothing but the score, the keyboard and at most two buttons.

### M1.1 · M · Timestamps and velocity on every played note (never cut) — done 20 Oct

- **As built:**
  - Session schema 2 stores, per attempt, `began_ms` and `notes`. Each note
    has `key`, `at_us`, `velocity` and `released_us`. The sustain pedal's
    changes are kept too.
  - Times are taken as an `Instant` when each message arrives, not from
    midir's timestamp, which restarts at 0 on an ALSA reconnect.
  - Schema 1 files still load, with their notes untimed.
  - Golden files in `crates/hogwash-records/tests/fixtures/` pin the
    format.
  - Replay is unchanged: it keeps no record, so timing it would show
    nothing.
  - Verified with `just e2e` (`scripts/e2e/`), the real-app test kit. It
    drives the packaged AppImage with a virtual MIDI keyboard; run it after
    any change in behaviour.
- **Why:** stall time per bar (M2) is impossible without them.
- **What:** midir's microsecond timestamp and the note-on velocity travel
  from the MIDI callback → practice thread → `ObservedPerformance` →
  `AttemptRecord` on disk.
- **How (outline):**
  - `hogwash-capture`: `ObservedPerformance` records `(pitch, micros, velocity)`
    instead of `pitch`. Keep `played()` returning pitches so existing
    analysis keeps working unchanged.
  - `hogwash-midi/src/keyboard.rs`: stop ignoring `_timestamp`; read velocity
    from the note-on message.
  - Records: add the fields with defaults so **old session files still load**
    (time and velocity unknown).
  - Engine stays pure: time is data supplied by the adapter, never read from
    a clock in the domain.
  - `just replay` event files: add optional `"t"` and `"v"` fields; files
    without them still work.
- **Tests:** an old record loads; a new record round-trips; replay with and
  without times gives identical findings; determinism check still passes.
- **Manual check:** play 8 notes, quit, open the newest session file, see
  increasing timestamps and non-zero velocities.

### M1.2 · S · Leaving the practice screen ends and saves the attempt — done 18 Oct

- **Why:** today the attempt keeps running in the background.
- **What:** "← Library", Esc, and opening another piece all call
  `end_attempt` first. The attempt is saved like any other.
- **Edge:** if nothing was played, don't save an empty session.
- **Manual check:** play 5 notes, press ← Library, reopen; history count +1.

### M1.3 · S · Remember the instrument; one-click start; visible device

- **Why:** picking the same keyboard every time is pure friction.
- **What:**
  - Remember the last MIDI input name. If it's connected, select it silently.
  - If exactly one device is connected, use it. Only show the picker when
    there are several, or when the remembered one is missing.
  - A device indicator in the header of both screens: green dot +
    short device name; grey dot + "No keyboard" when none. Clicking it opens
    the input/output settings.
  - **Auto-start:** with a device selected, the first correct note starts the
    attempt. No Start button needed. (Keep a visible "Start" for clarity on
    first use only; see §15.)
  - **Auto-detect (added 24 Oct):** while no keyboard is connected, look again
    every 3 seconds, on the library and the practice screen. When one appears,
    select it (the remembered one first) and show it at once: no going back,
    no re-opening the piece. While still empty, the "No MIDI keyboard" panel
    has a **Rescan** button. Unplugging mid-practice stays as it is (the
    one-minute reconnect window).
- **Manual check:** unplug/replug the FP-10 between launches; the app picks
  it up with zero clicks. Open the Minuet with the piano off, switch it on:
  within ~3 s it is picked up, with zero clicks.

### M1.4 · M · A summary you can read in five seconds — done 24 Oct

- **As built (24 Oct, third pass — the coach):** after your test ("graphs, but
  no real feedback") and research (Duke et al. 2009; r/piano, r/pianolearning;
  Chang; Morsi et al. SMC 2024; feedback-after-practice findings), the results
  lead with **at most three cards**: the bars cut out of your score (wrong
  notes ringed, pauses marked), what happened, why, 2–3 steps to fix it, and a
  button that starts the drill. Diagnoses (`hogwash-analysis/src/coach.rs`):
  going into a bar, a jump, fast notes (as a chord shape or a run), wrong
  notes (misread vs landed a key off), long notes cut short, bars that slow
  you down, stopping in the same bar, a piece that is a big step. One fumble
  is not a card; the same mistake every time through is. "Went well" chips;
  numbers and chart under Details. The run finishes by itself 1.5 s after the
  last note. Next: the long-term view of a piece (step 3), then key
  signatures and hands in the score data (step 4) for the reading and
  hand-coordination diagnoses.
- **As built (24 Oct, second pass after your test):** stopping swaps the score
  for a **results view** in the middle of the screen (Monkeytype-style; Esc
  shows the score with the run's marks, Enter takes the suggestion, R plays
  again). Big numbers: accuracy and tempo (♩/min); a chart of tempo per bar
  (per pass in a loop) with ✕ for wrong notes and a grey band for bars not
  reached; small numbers: notes right/wrong/not reached, steadiness, longest
  pause and its bar, time, restarts. It covers the whole **run** (Start to
  stopping: restarts, jumps, loop passes). Nothing sits above the score while
  practising: messages go to one status line in the header. Scores like
  accuracy % are allowed now (your call). e2e scenario `review`.
- **Why:** 517 lines is not feedback.
- **When:** only after playing (Stop, the end, or leaving), never during.
- **What:** ~80% picture, ~20% text, like Monkeytype's results screen:
  1. **On the score:** bars not reached greyed; every right note a green
     dot, every wrong one a red ✕, kept (not faded) until the next attempt.
  2. **A strip under the score:** one column per bar played, in played
     order. Column height = pause (M2.1), red dots = wrong notes, grey = not
     reached. Hover/click a column → that bar on the score.
  3. **One line of text:** *"Bars 1–18 · 6 min 40 s · most trouble going into bar 9."*
  4. **One button:** Loop the weakest spot (with the bar before it when the
     trouble is going into the bar) · plus Again · Library.
- Detailed per-note lines move behind a "Show every note" disclosure, closed
  by default.
- **Where:** numbers from `Bars` (M2.1) and grouping in `hogwash-feedback`
  (pure, tested); React only draws.

### M1.5 · M · Start from any bar (never cut) — done 22 Oct

- **As built:**
  - Session schema 3 adds, per attempt, `from` (the step it started at) and
    `ended` (finished, restart, jump, stop, left, lost). Older files load;
    a file with an unknown ending is skipped and logged.
  - A click goes to the bar's first occurrence in played order; a bar with
    nothing to strike starts at the next one. A note tied into the start
    bar is waited for.
  - The start point shows as a green "Start" marker, and resets to bar 1 on
    leaving the piece. Mid-attempt, a click keeps the attempt (`jump`).

- **Why:** you never want to play bars 1–8 again to practise bar 9.
- **What:** click a bar on the score → the cursor jumps to the first
  position of that bar and waits there.
- **How:** a new `Cursor` constructor/method that places it at the first
  position of a measure *in played order*. With repeats, a bar appears twice;
  pick the first occurrence after the current cursor, or the first overall if
  not practising.
- **UX:** hovering a bar shows a faint outline and a tooltip "Start here (bar 9)".
  The click target is the bar's whole area, not just the notes.
- **Tests:** replay from bar 9 of the Minuet; from bar 1 of the second half
  (repeat-aware).

### M1.6 · L · Loop a range of bars (never cut) — done 22 Oct

- **As built:**
  - Shift+click loops from the start bar to the bar clicked; Loop / L loops
    the cursor's bar; Esc, Loop again, a plain click or leaving ends it.
  - Each pass is its own attempt: schema 4 stores `"to"` (the loop's end,
    a step) and `"ended": "pass"`. A loop over a repeat sign is the first
    place its bars are played in a row.
  - "N clean in a row" (D11); a wrong note shows 0 at once. "stop at" 1–10
    or never, default 3, not yet remembered (M1.9). At the target: "3 clean
    passes · bars 9–12", the band goes, the cursor waits at the loop start.
  - Known limit: the key-up of a pass's last note lands in the next pass.

- **Why:** the single most effective practice technique, and the feature
  every competitor has.
- **What:**
  - Select: click a bar, Shift+click another → bars between are selected
    (a tinted band across both staves). Or use the toolbar's Loop button,
    which loops the bar the cursor is in.
  - While looping, reaching the end of the range wraps the cursor to the
    range start.
  - Counter in the toolbar: **"2 clean in a row"** (D11). A wrong or
    unexpected note resets it to 0 for that pass.
  - Optional target: "Stop after 3 clean passes" (default on, value
    editable: 1–10). When reached: a calm confirmation, loop ends, the cursor
    stays.
  - Esc or the Loop button clears the loop.
- **Where:** loop logic in `hogwash-practice` (a range on the attempt, wrap
  rule, clean-pass counter), fully unit-tested; UI only draws it.
- **Records:** each attempt stores its loop range, so stats know which bars
  were drilled.
- **Edge cases:** a range that crosses a repeat sign; a one-bar loop; a range
  ending on a tied note that continues into the next bar (the tie must not
  be waited for again on wrap).

### M1.7 · M · Hands separately (cut first)

- **What:** toolbar selector Both / Right / Left. Right = staff 1, Left =
  staff 2. The other staff is dimmed to ~35% opacity and its notes are not
  waited for. Notes played from the unselected hand are neither correct nor
  wrong (tolerated).
- **Where:** `hogwash-practice` filters expected notes by staff. Analysis
  must know which hand was practised so it doesn't count the other staff as
  "never played."
- **Edge:** pieces with one staff (Ode to Joy) hide the selector.

### M1.8 · S · Restart without leaving — done 18 Oct (bar 1); from the loop start since M1.6

- **What:** **R** restarts the attempt from the current loop start, or from
  bar 1 if no loop. Saves the current attempt first.

### M1.10 · M · Chords held together (next, after you test the results view)

- A chord step counts only when all its notes are **down at the same moment**;
  releasing one before the last arrives means pressing it again (not a mistake).
- Exempt: notes tied into it, grace notes, written arpeggios. The pedal isn't holding.
- The cursor needs releases (already recorded); analysis replays presses and
  releases in time order so screen and results agree. Old records replay leniently.
- Both modes.

### M1.11 · M · Learn / Play modes (after M1.10)

- Header toggle **Learn | Play**, remembered.
- **Learn:** the 3rd wrong note in the current bar sends the cursor back to the
  bar's start ("↺ bar 9" in the status line; tied-in notes struck, as when
  starting there). 4+ wrong in one bar in a run, note names off → once per run:
  "Bar 9 keeps tripping you — turn on note names? [Yes]". Inside a loop the
  restart goes to the bar's start and the pass isn't clean. Results show
  restarts per bar.
- **Play:** today's relaxed wait-for-me (with M1.10's chords).
- Bar restarts are recorded as attempts ending "restart" inside the run.

### M1.9 · S · Remember per-piece state

- **What:** per piece, remember last zoom, hands setting, loop range and the
  bar you stopped at. Reopening the piece restores them, with a quiet hint:
  *"Picking up at bar 9 · Start from bar 1"* (link).
- **Where:** a small per-piece settings file next to the piece record.

## 11. M2 — The differentiator: weak bars

**Goal:** Hogwash can tell you which bar to practise today, and you agree.
**Time:** ~1.5 weeks.

### Fixed definitions

Change these deliberately (log in §32), never by accident.

Built in M2.1 (`hogwash-analysis/src/bars.rs`, `Bars::of`):

- **Pace** = the attempt's median time per written quarter note. A slow day
  is not a hesitant one.
- **Pause** at a step = the time beyond 1.5 × what the written rhythm asks at
  that pace. A long note or rest is not a pause. The first step of an attempt
  or loop pass has none. Without a written rhythm every step counts as a quarter.
- **Into-bar pause** = the pause on a bar's first step: the change from the
  bar before (suggest looping the two together).
- **Break** = a gap over 20 s and over 10× what the music asks. Counted, not a pause.
- **Bar wrong notes** = wrong notes while the cursor waited in the bar, **at
  most 3 per step** (hunting for one note is one problem). Grace notes and
  ornaments excluded. Brushed neighbour keys count (your call, 24 Oct).
- **Gave up** = the attempt ended by Restart or Stop while in this bar.
- **Passes** = times through the bar; a repeat's bars count once per time through.
- **Bar difficulty** (`Bar::difficulty`, ranking only, never shown) = per
  pass: pause seconds + wrong notes × 1 + gave up × 2. Tune later.
- **Baseline** = average over the last 3 sessions of that piece in which the
  bar was played (D10).
- A bar needs at least **2 passes** of data before it gets a colour; until
  then it's "not enough data yet." Bars never reached have no data, not "easy".

## 15. UX blueprint: making it frictionless

### 15.1 The friction principle

> **From launching Hogwash to hearing your first note judged: at most
> 2 clicks. From finishing a pass to starting the next: 0 clicks.**

Every flow below has a click budget. A change that adds a click needs a reason
written in §32.

### 15.2 Today's friction audit (6 Oct)

Launch → play the Minuet, as the code works now:

| Step | Today | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Open app | Library | Library (or the last piece, see 15.3) |
| Open piece | 1 click | 1 click |
| Choose instrument | 1 click, every time | 0 (remembered / auto) |
| Press Start | 1 click | 0 (first correct note starts) |
| Practise bar 9 | Play bars 1–8 first | 1 click on bar 9 |
| Loop bars 9–12 | Not possible | Click + Shift-click |
| Try again | 1 click "Again" in summary | 0 (loop wraps) or **R** |
| Back to library | 1 click (attempt keeps running) | 1 click or Esc (attempt saved) |
| See where you struggled | Read a list of lines | Heatmap on the score |

### 15.3 Core flows and click budgets

**Flow A — First launch ever (budget: 3 clicks to playing)**
1. Library opens with bundled pieces and a first-run card.
2. If no keyboard: onboarding strip (connect → it's detected automatically →
   ready). If one keyboard: header shows it, nothing to do.
3. Click "Minuet in G" → the score opens, cursor on the first note, a quiet
   line: *"Play the first note to begin."*
4. Play. That's it.

**Flow B — Daily practice (budget: 1 click)**
- Library shows a **"Continue"** card at the top: last piece, the bar you
  stopped at, and its weakest bars. One click → practice screen, same loop
  and hands as last time.
- Option (decide in M3): a setting "Open the last piece on launch" (default off).

**Flow C — Fix a hard bar (budget: 2 clicks)**
- On the score, click the darkest bar → bar card → **Loop this bar**.
- Or toolbar → **Practise weak bars** (1 click, Hogwash chooses).

**Flow D — Import a piece (budget: 2 clicks, or a drag)**
- Library → **Add a piece** → file dialog → it opens straight into practice.
- Or drag the file onto the window.
- Duplicate → *"Already in your library"* and opens it (exists today).

**Flow E — Keyboard unplugged mid-practice (budget: 0 clicks)**
- Banner: *"FP-10 disconnected. Plug it back in to keep going — your
  progress is kept."* Reconnect → banner fades, cursor still where it was.
  (The grace-period logic exists; the copy and the "keep going" behaviour
  are the work.)

**Flow F — End of a session (budget: 0–1 clicks)**
- Leaving or stopping always saves. The summary appears inline above the
  score; it never blocks. **Again** is the default (Enter).

### 15.4 Practice screen states

The practice screen is a small state machine. Every state has one obvious
next action.

| State | What you see | Obvious next action |
|---|---|---|
| **Ready** | Score, heatmap on, cursor at start/last bar, "Play the first note to begin" | Play a note |
| **Playing** | Heatmap off, toolbar dimmed, cursor + position band, live marks | Keep playing |
| **Looping** | Range band, "2 clean in a row" counter in the toolbar | Keep playing; Esc to stop looping |
| **Loop done** | Short confirmation: "3 clean passes · bars 9–12" | Next weak bar / keep looping / stop |
| **Device lost** | Banner with countdown; cursor frozen | Plug the keyboard back in |
| **Ended** | Inline summary above the score, heatmap back on | Again (Enter) |

### 15.5 Keyboard shortcuts

Shortcuts must never fire while typing in a text field (rename, search).

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| Space | Start / stop the attempt |
| R | Restart from loop start (or bar 1) |
| L | Loop the current bar; with a selection, loop the selection; again to stop |
| ← / → | Move the cursor one bar back / forward (when not mid-chord) |
| Home | Go to bar 1 |
| H | Cycle hands: Both → Right → Left |
| W | Toggle weak-bar heatmap |
| Y | History panel |
| N | Toggle note names |
| + / − / 0 | Zoom in / out / reset |
| Esc | Clear selection → stop loop → close panel → back to library (in that order) |
| ? | Shortcut overlay |
| Ctrl+O | Add a piece |
| Ctrl+F | Search the library |

### 15.6 Small conveniences worth having (all v1.0 unless marked)

- The window remembers its size and position.
- The last-used zoom per piece (M1.9).
- Hover on any bar shows its number (helpful in long pieces).
- Bar numbers on every system start (Verovio option), not just every 5.
- Clicking the device indicator opens MIDI settings directly.
- Rename a piece by double-clicking its title (library).
- Undo for delete: a 6-second "Deleted · Undo" toast instead of a
  confirmation dialog.
- "Copy summary" button in the end panel (plain text, for sending to a teacher).

### 16.3 Type

| Role | Font | Size token |
|---|---|---|
| Display (library heading) | Serif | `display` 4.25 rem |

| Numbers, bar counts, timers | Mono, tabular figures | as context |

Only the scale in `index.css` is allowed. No one-off sizes.

### 16.4 Spacing, shape, elevation

- Spacing on a 4 px grid: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48.
- Radius: 8 px controls, 12 px cards, 16 px panels, full for pills.
- Elevation: two levels only (raised panel, popover). Shadows soft and low.

### 16.5 Motion

- Durations: 120 ms (hover, press), 160 ms (panels), 200 ms (screens).
- One easing: ease-out. No spring bounce, no stagger longer than 50 ms.
- Score never animates position on reflow (it's hidden until fitted, as now).
- Reduced motion: all transitions become fades or instant.

### 16.6 Icons

- One icon set, 16/20 px, 1.5–2 px stroke, inline SVG (no icon dependency
  unless it's tiny and tree-shakeable, e.g. Lucide).
- Needed: gear, library, loop, hands, clock (history), weak bars
  (a simple target), keyboard, play/stop, restart, zoom ±,
  question mark.

## 18. Words: microcopy and feedback language

### 18.1 Voice

- Plain, calm, specific. Like a good teacher's pencil note in the margin.
- Second person, present tense. Short sentences.
- Say *where* and *what*, never *how good*.
- British/US spelling: pick one (currently British: "practise" as verb,
  "practice" as noun). Keep it consistent.

### 18.2 Do / don't

| Situation | Don't | Do |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong note | "Oops! Wrong note!" | (no text; the mark on the staff says it) |
| End of attempt | "Great job! 87% accuracy!" | "Bars 1–18 played · 3 bars to work on" |
| Weak bar | "You're bad at bar 9" | "Bar 9 · average pause 4.2 s · 2 wrong notes per pass" |
| Improvement | "Amazing progress!!" | "Bar 9: average pause 14.1 s → 3.2 s over 6 sessions" |
| No device | "ERROR: No MIDI device found" | "No keyboard connected. Plug it in — Hogwash will find it." |
| Bad file | "Parse error at line 412" | "This file couldn't be read as sheet music. If it came from MuseScore, try exporting it again as MusicXML." |
| Loop done | "🎉 Level up!" | "3 clean passes · bars 9–12" |

### 18.3 Glossary (use these words, consistently)

- **piece** (not song, not score, in UI), **bar** (not measure, in UI),
  **attempt** (one play-through), **session** (one sitting with a piece),
  **loop**, **clean pass**, **weak bars**, **pause** (user-facing word for
  stall), **keyboard** (the MIDI instrument), **library**.

## 19. Errors, edge cases and empty states

### 19.1 Error rules

1. Every error says **what happened** and **what to do next**, in plain words.
2. Internals (`UnhandledPromise`, stack traces, paths) never reach the main
   UI. Put them behind a "Details" disclosure, copyable for bug reports.
3. A failure in one feature never takes down the screen. (Today, any thrown
   command error replaces the whole window with the Unavailable screen —
   keep that only for "Hogwash can't start at all.")
4. Errors that fix themselves (device back) disappear on their own.

### 19.2 Edge cases to handle explicitly

| Case | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Two MIDI keyboards connected | Use the remembered one; picker in settings |
| Keyboard reconnects with a different port name | Match by name prefix; ask only if ambiguous |
| Sustain pedal (CC64) | Ignored for judging; never counts as a wrong note |
| Notes held from the previous position | Not re-judged (ties logic already covers written ties) |
| Piece with > 2 staves or several parts | Refuse clearly on import for v1.0: "Hogwash handles one piano part (two staves)." |
| Piece with a pickup (anacrusis) bar | Bar numbering must match the printed score (test with a PD Für Elise) |
| Very long piece (300+ bars) | Engraving and history stay within budgets (§21) |
| Mid-bar clef change | Known limit (§27) |
| Played an octave off everywhere | Octave-shift hint (M3.14) |
| Piece needs keys the keyboard lacks | Warn on open (M3.13) |
| Session file corrupted on disk | Skip it, keep the rest, note it in Details |
| Disk full / no write permission | "Couldn't save this session (disk full?). You can keep playing." |
| App closed mid-attempt | Attempt saved on window close (extend M1.2) |
| Computer sleeps mid-attempt | On wake, treat like a device reconnect |

### 19.3 Empty states (all need copy and one action)

- No pieces imported yet (library section)
- Piece never practised (heatmap toggle, history panel)
- Not enough data for a bar (bar card: "Play this bar twice to see stats.")
- No keyboard connected (header + practice status line)
- History panel with one session ("Practise again to see changes over time.")

## 20. Accessibility

Not a polish item — a correctness item. Minimum bar for v1.0:

- Every control reachable by keyboard, in a sensible order; visible focus
  ring (accent, 2 px, offset 2 px — already the pattern).
- Every icon button has an `aria-label`; toggles use `aria-pressed`;
  the hands selector is a radio group (the instrument picker already is).
- Live region (`role="status"`) for: attempt started, loop pass counter,
  loop done, device lost/restored. Throttled so it doesn't chatter.
- Colour never the only signal (§16.2).
- Text scales with the OS setting; layout survives 150% UI zoom.
- Reduced motion honoured everywhere.
- Hit targets ≥ 32 px for toolbar controls.

## 23. Content and licensing

### 23.1 Rules

1. A public-domain **composition** is not a public-domain **file**. Every
   bundled file needs its own licence recorded in `PROVENANCE.md`.
2. Never commit a score you can't redistribute, even temporarily (git
   history is forever). Test scores live outside the repo.
3. Never download from MuseScore.com with tools that bypass its paywall.
4. Arrangements of copyrighted songs are never bundled. Users import their own.

## 25. After v1.0 — the ordered plan

Don't start any of these before v1.0 ships. Order may change based on what
users ask for; log the change in §32.

### Next 1 · Tempo mode and a metronome
- A bar that's clean in wait mode can **graduate** to tempo practice: the
  cursor moves at a set tempo; notes are judged early/late/on time.
- Metronome click: send it to the piano as MIDI (a rim-click or woodblock
  note on channel 10), not through a software audio stack.
- Tempo ladder for a loop: start at 60%, +5% after each clean pass, back
  5% after a failed one.
- Timing stats join the bar card ("rushes the third beat").

### Next 2 · Listen
- Play the score (or the loop range, or one hand) so you can hear how it
  goes: the cursor follows and each note lights up as it sounds; Space stops.
- Sound: **see D13** (your piano over MIDI, a built-in piano sound, or both).

### Next 3 · MIDI file import
- Via webmscore in the webview, behind `midiToMusicXml(bytes)` (design in
  `MIDI-IMPORT.md`). Requires the licence decision (D2): GPL-3.0 + bundle
  is the simplest route.
- Before building: run the 15-file conversion test (sequenced, performance-
  captured and full-band MIDIs) and grade A/B/C. Go only if ≥ 10/15 are A or B.
- Clear messaging: "Converted from MIDI — notation may need cleanup."

### Next 4 · `.mscz` import
- Same webmscore path; no conversion loss. Covers people's own MuseScore.com
  downloads.

### Next 5 · A daily practice plan across pieces
- "Today, 15 min: Minuet bars 9–12 (5 min) · Prelude bars 21–24 (5 min) ·
  play the Minuet through once (5 min)."
- Spaced review: bars that were weak a week ago come back.
- Still no streaks, no scores.

### Next 6 · Windows build (then macOS)
- CI already runs on Windows. Bundling Verovio as a `.dll` is the work.
- macOS needs signing/notarisation; only if there's demand.

### Next 7 · Dynamics feedback
- Velocity is already captured (D9). Compare against written dynamics
  (p, f, crescendo) per bar: "Bar 12 marked p · you played mf."

### Next 8 · Practice notes per bar
- A short text note attached to a bar ("thumb under on the F#"), shown in
  the bar card.

### Next 9 · Fingering
- Show fingering from MusicXML (Verovio already draws it); toggle on/off.

### Next 10 · Session report export
- PDF or Markdown of a week's practice, for a teacher.

### Later (unordered)
- One-page website (GitHub Pages) with the video.
- More bundled pieces (OpenScore, OpenWTC Book I in full).
- Accompaniment for one hand while you play the other (MIDI out).
- Page-turn mode (no scrolling; full page flips) for people who prefer it.
- Multiple keyboards / duet mode.
- Translations.
- Flatpak / AUR package.
- Practice-time reminders (local notification, opt-in).
- Import from a folder (watch a directory of MusicXML).
- 2026-10-18 · Feature ideas (click to seek, play button, on-screen keyboard, smooth scroll, report, layouts, ...): sorted in `FEATURE-IDEAS.md`.
- Simplify a piece (melody only / reduce chords) — only if it can be done
  honestly.

## 27. Known limits (fix when one actually bites)

Carried over from the previous roadmap; each one is real but rare.

- **Upward-resolving trill** (F trilled with G, then G) advances the cursor
  on its first G; the rest of the trill counts as wrong. The ornament
  tolerance covers only the trilled step and the next one.
- **Tied-to notes and grace notes get no note-name label** (they aren't marks).
- **At a repeat**, the cursor slides back across the page and nothing shows
  which pass (1st/2nd time) you're on.
- **A clef change mid-bar** takes effect from the next bar in score geometry
  (no bundled piece has one).
- **Wrong-note mark's accidental** ignores accidentals earlier in the same bar.
- **Re-importing an edited file** creates a new piece (new fingerprint); the
  old history stays with the old version. Want: "replace file, keep history."
- **Closing the window mid-attempt** loses the attempt (leaving the practice
  screen now saves it; the window close does not).
- **A zip damaged inside an entry** (not cut short) could still reach Verovio
  and crash it; only the container's structure is checked.
- **A real Für Elise with a pickup bar** hasn't been verified; test bar
  numbering with a public-domain source (don't commit the test file).
- Promoted out of this list into milestones: the 517-line early stop (M1.4),
  the attempt that keeps running (M1.2), the unreachable-keys wait (M3.13),
  the octave-shift buttons (M3.14).

## 28. Never — and why

| Never | Why |
|---|---|
| Accounts, cloud sync, telemetry | Breaks local-first; adds servers to run and data to protect |
| XP, levels, ranks, streaks, points, badges | Optimises for opening the app, not for playing better |
| Accuracy percentages as the headline | One number hides *where*; bars are the unit of practice |
| Flashcards, theory drills, lessons, exams, games | MidiSlayer's lane; we win on depth with pieces |
| Falling notes as a mode | Synthesia/Neothesia's lane; Hogwash is about reading real notation |

| Bundling copyrighted arrangements | Legal risk to the whole repo |
| An in-app store or content marketplace | Not a business; not the product |
| AI-generated practice advice | Factual stats are explainable and deterministic; an LLM is neither (and needs a network) |

## 29. Mistakes to avoid

From this project's own history, and from what we saw in other projects.
Ordered by how likely you are to make each one.

1. **Polishing before the loop exists.** A beautiful screen around a missing
   core. M1 and M2 come before M3 for this reason.
2. **Copying a competitor's breadth.** MidiSlayer looks complete because it
   does many things. You win by doing one thing better.
3. **Researching when motivation dips.** You already researched the wrong
   thing once ("can I render notation?" instead of "does the content exist?").
   When stuck: practise with the app for 15 minutes and write annoyances.
4. **Rewriting the stack.** It already happened once (spec-first → Tauri).
   Rust core + Tauri + Verovio works. No framework changes before v1.0.
5. **Unpushed work.** Six weeks once lived only on one disk. Push daily.
6. **Committing files you can't redistribute.** Git history is forever.
7. **Letting this file become a spec.** Lists, sizes, "done when." No ADRs,
   no FR-numbers, no governance.
8. **Letting Claude Code redesign freely.** One screen per task; plan →
   approve → build; manual checklist at the end.
9. **Adding gamification to fix your own motivation.** Streaks help the
   builder feel good about metrics; they don't help the player.
10. **Starting MIDI import early because it's exciting.** It's post-v1.0
    and needs a licence decision. The weak-bars feature is the product.
11. **Moving dates instead of cutting features.** Cut from the bottom.
12. **Judging success by stars or downloads.** See §30.
13. **Building for imaginary users.** Every feature should trace back to
    `annoyances.md`, a real user's hesitation, or the one sentence.
14. **Big-bang changes.** Each task should leave the app working. If a task
    breaks the app for more than a day, it was too big.

## 30. Motivation and sustainability

### Success, defined in advance

Hogwash v1.0 is a success if all three are true, regardless of stars:
1. You practise with it **5+ days a week** for a month after launch.
2. **At least one stranger** opens an issue, sends a message, or says it helped.
3. You can explain **every design decision** in your own words.

### On low-motivation days

- **The minimum day:** practise piano with Hogwash for 15 minutes and add
  one line to `annoyances.md`. That counts as a project day.
- **The smallest task:** pick an S task from the active milestone. Finish it.
  Push. Stop.
- **Don't** research competitors, read about new frameworks, or redesign
  this file on low days.

### Doubts

- New doubts about the project go in a `## Doubts` section of
  `annoyances.md` with a date. They're reviewed at the end of each milestone,
  not acted on mid-milestone.

### Remember why

- MuseScore's founder nearly lost interest once his first version worked;
  he came back and it outlasted Finale.
- Lichess survived because one stranger used it and complained about bugs.
- MidiSlayer charges $50 to practise a public-domain Bach prelude. You're
  building the version anyone can use.
